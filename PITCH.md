## Slide 2 — The Problem

**Woodworkers run their business on duct tape.**

- Quotes scrawled on yellow notepads, lost in glove compartments
- Cut lists calculated by hand at the saw — math errors waste lumber
- Customer info scattered across iMessage threads, voicemails, and emails
- Invoices typed up at 9pm in Microsoft Word, sometimes forgotten
- The average solo woodworker loses **4–6 hours per week** to admin

*Speaker notes: This pain isn't theoretical — every woodworker we've talked to nods at the first bullet. The opportunity isn't a software gap; it's the gap between what trades professionals already do manually every day and what the tools they pay for actually deliver.*

---

## Slide 3 — Why Now

Three concurrent shifts make this the moment:

1. **Mobile-native trade workforce.** 89% of US tradespeople under 40 already run their business primarily from their phone. The buyer is finally ready.
2. **Boomer-to-millennial tool handoff.** Spreadsheets and paper are out; expectation of polished mobile UX is in.
3. **Custom woodworking demand surge.** Home-improvement spending is at a 15-year high, and custom furniture / cabinetry has outpaced volume builders for 6 quarters running.

*Speaker notes: The "why now" is the wedge between worker expectation and software quality. Existing apps for trades feel like 2012 Salesforce; the new buyer expects something that feels like Notion or Linear.*

## Slide 5 — Solution

**One app. Whole shop.**

- **Cut list generator** that optimizes sheet stock
- **Board-foot calculator** for hardwood pricing
- **Quote builder** with markup and signature capture
- **Invoices** with paid/unpaid tracking
- **Client CRM** with full project history
- **Tool inventory** with maintenance reminders
- **Photo & shop drawing manager** with before/after sliders
- **Works fully offline** — syncs when back online

*Speaker notes: Other apps do one of these things in isolation. WoodWorks Pro is the first to combine them in a workflow built around how woodworkers actually move through a project.*

**Paid up front. Optional add-ons. No mandatory subscription.**

| Product | Price | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Cloud Sync | $4.99/mo or $39.99/yr | Subscription |
| CNC File Manager | $14.99 | One-time IAP |
| Team Collaboration | $9.99/mo per seat | Subscription |

### Unit Economics (Year 1 targets)
- CAC: $4 (via maker YouTubers + Instagram, no paid ads in v1)
- LTV (base app only): $9.99
- LTV (with 30% Cloud Sync attach): $50+
- Gross margin: 70% after App Store cut
- Payback period: < 1 month

*Speaker notes: We are intentionally **not** going subscription-only. Woodworkers are tool buyers — they expect to own what they buy. The subscription is for what they want servers to do (sync, team), not for permission to use the app.*

## Slide 8 — Go-to-Market

Three channels, in priority order:

1. **Maker YouTubers & Instagram woodworkers.** Authentic-feel partnerships with channels at 50K–500K subs. Target: 8 launch partners, ~3M total reach.
2. **Trade-association co-marketing.** Woodworking Network, AWFS, IWF, and regional cabinet maker associations. Bundled discounts, conference demos.
3. **App Store organic + ASO.** Keyword strategy targets "cut list", "board foot", "woodworking" — verticals with low competition and high intent.

Paid acquisition is a Phase 3 lever, not a launch lever.

*Speaker notes: We win on credibility, not budget. A YouTuber whose audience trusts them mentioning the app does more than a $20K Facebook campaign would.*

## Appendix — FAQ for Investor Questions

These are likely follow-up questions. Pre-write your answers; do not improvise on stage.

**Q: Why not target general contractors? Bigger market.**
A: Bigger but vastly more crowded. We win woodworkers because no one else is fighting for them, then expand category-by-category (closet builders → millwork → small commercial cabinet) once we own the wedge.

**Q: Why paid up-front instead of free + subscription?**
A: Two reasons. (1) Woodworkers are tool buyers — they pay for tools they own; subscriptions feel like rent and produce buyer-resentment in this segment. (2) Paid app filters for serious users, which compounds into better reviews and ASO ranking.

**Q: What if the App Store kills your distribution?**
A: We're not a discovery-dependent product. 60%+ of our launch traffic comes from owned channels (YouTuber partnerships, association co-marketing). App Store is a checkout funnel, not the audience.

**Q: How defensible is this against a fast follower?**
A: Three moats: (1) brand credibility with woodworkers earned through partnerships, (2) accumulated material/pricing library that improves with usage, (3) integration depth with CNC files and finishing workflows that takes 12+ months to replicate. None are unbreachable individually; together they're hard to leapfrog.

**Q: How do you grow beyond solo woodworkers?**
A: Team Collaboration is the natural upsell at 2–10 employees, and it makes us **stickier** at the shop level. Beyond that, the web companion in Phase 3 unlocks owner-operators who manage from a desk, not a saw.
